🏋️‍♂️ The Squat: The 5 AbsolutesDr. Horschig states that while hip anatomy dictates exact stance width, everyone must follow five foundational rules:Toe Angle: Point toes relatively straight forward. A slight 5–7 degree flare is acceptable.Tripod Foot: Distribute weight evenly across three points: the heel, base of the big toe, and base of the little toe.Hip Hinge: Initiate the movement by driving the hips back slightly and tilting the chest forward to engage the posterior chain.External Rotation Torque: Actively root your feet into the floor, squeeze your glutes, and drive your knees outward so they track in line with your toes.Postural Integrity: Keep a neutral spine and a locked core. Descend until your thighs drop below parallel.The Ascent: Drive through your heels to push straight up, ensuring your hips and chest rise at the exact same rate.🛏️ The Bench Press: Powerlifting Tripod & Shoulder HealthTo maximize power and protect the shoulders, the bench press must be treated as a full-body movement.The Setup: Create a stable tripod by digging your upper traps and heels firmly into the ground while keeping your butt lightly grazing the bench.Lats Engagement: Grip the bar tightly and try to physically "bend the bar" with your hands. This packs your shoulder blades back and down, engaging the lats.The Descent: Unrack by pushing into the bar with 10–20% effort first to establish stability. Lower the bar with a slow, controlled negative to your lower sternum, keeping your elbows tucked at roughly a 45-degree angle.The Press: Leg drive is initiated by pushing your heels into the floor, routing that force through your hamstrings and glutes into the upper body to propel the bar upward.🧱 The Deadlift: Two Movements in OneSquat University outlines the deadlift as a combination of a squat and a Romanian Deadlift (RDL), broken into distinct "pillars".The Setup: Stand with feet inside shoulder width. Position the bar directly over your mid-foot so it is touching or nearly touching your shins.The Wedge: Hinge down to grab the bar. Pull the "slack" out of the bar by pulling your chest up and lats down. Lengthen your arms ("think about flexing your triceps") to lock your upper back into slight thoracic extension.Floor to Knee (The Squat): Push the floor away through your mid-foot. Your hips and chest must rise simultaneously, maintaining the exact same back angle relative to the floor until the bar passes your knees.Knee to Lockout (The Hinge): Once the bar clears your kneecaps, drive your hips forward and squeeze your glutes hard to stand up completely straight.The Descent: Reverse the motion precisely. Hinge at the hips first (RDL) until the bar clears your knees, then squat the weight down to the floor.🏛️ The Overhead Press (OHP): Shoulder and Scapular StabilityDr. Horschig emphasizes that vertical pressing requires exceptional core bracing and active shoulder blade rotation to prevent impingement.The Stance & Grip: Stand with feet shoulder-width apart. Grip the bar just outside your shoulders so your forearms remain perfectly vertical under the bar.The Front Rack: Rest the bar on your anterior deltoids with your elbows pointing slightly forward (not flared out to the sides).The Core Brace: Squeeze your glutes and brace your abs intensely to lock your pelvis. This prevents your lower back from over-arching during the lift.The Bar Path: Pull your chin back slightly to clear a path for the bar. Press the barbell straight up in a perfectly vertical line, keeping it aligned over your mid-foot.The Lockout: Push your head forward ("pushing your head through the window") once the bar clears your forehead. Lock your elbows out fully at the top and shrug your shoulders upward slightly to maximize scapular stability.📊 Quick Form SummaryLiftPrimary CueBar AlignmentCore PrincipleSquat"Tripod foot & drive knees out"Over mid-footHip hinge first, maintain neutral spine.Bench"Bend the bar & use leg drive"Touch lower sternumPack shoulder blades back and down.Deadlift"Pull slack out & push the floor"Against the shinsSquat to the knees, hinge to the top.OHP"Brace glutes & push head through"Vertical path over mid-footKeep a rigid trunk to protect the lumbar spine.
